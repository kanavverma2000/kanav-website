Every five minutes, Australia's electricity market writes down what it just did. Who generated. What it cost. Who got paid.

Seventeen years of that. Nobody argues about the receipt.

They argue about what isn't on it.

Would that farm have earned more if the grid hadn't switched it off? Did closing that power station lift your bill, or did something else?

Everyone has an opinion. Nobody has the run of history where it didn't happen.

Ten of ten. The headline fell $66.06 against AEMO's stated −$66.

Why it isn't trivial AEMO's headline is a time-weighted average over five-minute dispatch periods. Its separate volume-weighted series is weighted by initial supply plus total intermittent generation: not total demand. Use total demand, the obvious guess, and you run $1–2/MWh high in every region. That one wrong choice quietly invalidates any capture benchmark built on it.
What it does

Eight questions.
And where each answer stops.

01, Curtailment attribution

Your farm was switched off. Was it the grid, or was it the price?

Measured against each unit's own unconstrained forecast, then split by cause. Either the network stopped you, or you chose not to sell.

Detail and limits

Measured against each unit's unconstrained forecast, then split by cause. A semi-dispatch cap means the network stopped you. Everything else is you responding to price. Network has been flat near 1 TWh a year since 2021; economic is up 420%.

Limit. The split is attributed over the capped subset: 7.60 of 8.37 TWh in 2025. The rest stays unattributed rather than guessed. No behind-the-meter.

02, Revenue capture

The market price is not the price you got.

Your generation-weighted price against the region's time-weighted average, per unit, at five-minute resolution.

Detail and limits

Generation-weighted price over the region's time-weighted average, per unit, at five-minute resolution across 22.3M records. Use the volume-weighted denominator instead and you understate every renewable asset in the market.

Limit. Both halves move. Fleet solar capture "recovered" 41.9% → 58.7% in H1 2026 while earnings fell $47.38 → $40.18/MWh. I always show both.

03, Counterfactual engine

Run the day again with one power station deleted. The difference is what it was worth.

169 units priced from AEMO's own heat rates, fuel prices and variable O&M, then cleared with and without.

Detail and limits

169 units priced from AEMO's own heat rates, fuel prices and variable O&M. Clear the stack with and without a unit. Then do it again at what participants actually bid, over a 227-unit stack. The gap between the two bounds is the markup.

Limit. The conduct bound's level is unvalidated: $43.77 modelled vs $94.84 observed, corr 0.415. Only the mean is readable. Published as a range.

04, Coal exit

Every coal unit has a closing date. Here is what the price does when each one goes.

Retirement schedule, closure history, fleet outlook and interconnector flows, across 45 units and 21.1 GW.

Detail and limits

Retirement schedule, closure history, station context, fleet outlook, interconnector flows and supply–demand balance. Closure impact runs through the counterfactual engine, not a rule of thumb.

Limit. Announced dates are policy statements, not physics, and they have moved. I report the schedule and model sensitivity to it. I don't forecast whether a date holds.

05, Storage economics

Most batteries are built four hours long. Most of the events they were bought for last longer.

Half a battery's value is invisible to a price spread. Charge, discharge, cycling, utilisation, strategy clustering, offtake.

Detail and limits

Charge, discharge, cycling, utilisation, price heatmaps, strategy clustering, offtake. Scarcity events run a mean of 7.3 hours and 84.7% exceed four, so a four-hour battery empties partway through most events it was bought for. $287,776/MW/yr at 4h, $442,879 at 8h.

Limit. The 8-hour result survives a capex scenario switch, but I no longer call it "the optimum", switching scenarios zeroes a whole 19 GW four-hour fleet.

06, Weather & resource

One bad year in thirty‑six decides whether a project works. Here is that year.

Hourly wind and solar rebuilt for 286 projects and 50,168 MW, 1990 to 2025, so the bad year is in the sample.

Detail and limits

Hourly wind and solar rebuilt for 286 projects, 50,168 MW, 1990–2025, calibrated to the unconstrained forecast rather than metered output, metered is already depressed by curtailment, so calibrating to it bakes the curtailment in.

Limit. Calibration is regional, not per-site: five scalars serve 286 projects and between-site variation runs 0.935–1.331.

07, System model

What should we build next? The honest answer depends on a number nobody knows.

Least-cost expansion across five regions and six interconnectors, with transmission priced inside the optimisation rather than bolted on.

Detail and limits

Least-cost expansion across five regions and six interconnectors, plus pumped hydro, hydrogen storage and a reserve margin. Interconnector limits derived from AEMO's own dispatch-interval limits against eight years of realised flow: not asserted.

Limit. The capacity mix is a function of the capex scenario, not a result. Switching published scenarios moves solar −41%, wind +61%, and a 19 GW battery fleet to zero. Mine included.

08, Scenarios & forecast error

Change one assumption and watch the market move. You will see which assumption I changed.

The merit-order stack re-cleared under changed inputs, with every assumption exposed and versioned.

Detail and limits

Scenarios are the merit-order stack re-cleared under changed inputs, every assumption exposed and versioned. Alongside it, a decomposition of where AEMO's own pre-dispatch goes wrong. Spikes are ~99% of five-minute error.

Limit. My spike classifier beats a naive "pre-dispatch above $300" rule by 1.1%. So I don't sell it. The bias correction is the part that survives.

Scope

What it does, and what it
won't pretend to do.

Does

  • Per-unit curtailment with an economic-versus-network cause split
  • Merit-order counterfactual clearing, cost and conduct bounds published separately
  • Per-unit revenue capture at five-minute granularity, against the correct time-weighted denominator
  • Closure impact from per-unit costs traced to AEMO's own workbook cells
  • 36 weather years of reconstructed wind and solar across 286 projects
  • Five-region capacity expansion with transmission priced inside the optimisation
  • Battery dispatch, cycling and event-based valuation
  • Scenario price paths with pre-dispatch bias correction

Doesn't

  • No AI price forecasting: the scenario engine is an explicit merit-order clear with published assumptions
  • No forward loss-factor product, that ground is already well occupied
  • No spike-prediction edge, ours beats a naive rule by 1.1%, so it isn't sold
  • No real-time monitoring, dedicated intraday tools do that better
  • No automated bidding, running an optimiser and benchmarking one is a conflict
  • No Western Australian coverage
  • No interval-level accuracy on the conduct clear, only the mean is readable
