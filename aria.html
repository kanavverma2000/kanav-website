What it does

Eight modules. Every one
ships its own limit.

01 — Curtailment attribution

Not how much was lost. Why.

Measured against each unit's unconstrained forecast, then split by cause. A semi-dispatch cap means the network stopped you. Everything else is you responding to price. Network has been flat near 1 TWh a year since 2021; economic is up 420%.

Limit. The split is attributed over the capped subset — 7.60 of 8.37 TWh in 2025. The rest stays unattributed rather than guessed. No behind-the-meter.
02 — Revenue capture

Against the right denominator.

Generation-weighted price over the region's time-weighted average, per unit, at five-minute resolution across 22.3M records. Use the volume-weighted denominator instead and you understate every renewable asset in the market.

Limit. Both halves move. Fleet solar capture "recovered" 41.9% → 58.7% in H1 2026 while earnings fell $47.38 → $40.18/MWh. I always show both.
03 — Counterfactual engine

Clear it twice. Report the difference.

169 units priced from AEMO's own heat rates, fuel prices and variable O&M. Clear the stack with and without a unit. Then do it again at what participants actually bid, over a 227-unit stack. The gap between the two bounds is the markup.

Limit. The conduct bound's level is unvalidated — $43.77 modelled vs $94.84 observed, corr 0.415. Only the mean is readable. Published as a range.
04 — Coal exit

45 units. 21.1 GW. What's left after.

Retirement schedule, closure history, station context, fleet outlook, interconnector flows and supply–demand balance. Closure impact runs through the counterfactual engine, not a rule of thumb.

Limit. Announced dates are policy statements, not physics, and they have moved. I report the schedule and model sensitivity to it. I don't forecast whether a date holds.
05 — Storage economics

Half a battery's value is invisible to a spread.

Charge, discharge, cycling, utilisation, price heatmaps, strategy clustering, offtake. Scarcity events run a mean of 7.3 hours and 84.7% exceed four — so a four-hour battery empties partway through most events it was bought for. $287,776/MW/yr at 4h, $442,879 at 8h.

Limit. The 8-hour result survives a capex scenario switch, but I no longer call it "the optimum" — switching scenarios zeroes a whole 19 GW four-hour fleet.
06 — Weather & resource

36 years, so the drought year shows up.

Hourly wind and solar rebuilt for 286 projects, 50,168 MW, 1990–2025, calibrated to the unconstrained forecast rather than metered output — metered is already depressed by curtailment, so calibrating to it bakes the curtailment in.

Limit. Calibration is regional, not per-site: five scalars serve 286 projects and between-site variation runs 0.935–1.331.
07 — System model

Five regions, transmission priced inside.

Least-cost expansion across five regions and six interconnectors, plus pumped hydro, hydrogen storage and a reserve margin. Interconnector limits derived from AEMO's own dispatch-interval limits against eight years of realised flow — not asserted.

Limit. The capacity mix is a function of the capex scenario, not a result. Switching published scenarios moves solar −41%, wind +61%, and a 19 GW battery fleet to zero. Mine included.
08 — Scenarios & forecast error

An explicit clear. Not a black box.

Scenarios are the merit-order stack re-cleared under changed inputs, every assumption exposed and versioned. Alongside it, a decomposition of where AEMO's own pre-dispatch goes wrong. Spikes are ~99% of five-minute error.

Limit. My spike classifier beats a naive "pre-dispatch above $300" rule by 1.1%. So I don't sell it. The bias correction is the part that survives.

Where it fits

Three questions.
Two are crowded.

Every product in this market answers one of three questions. Two are served by mature, well-funded incumbents and there's no point competing there. The third is the one people actually get asked in board papers.

Past tense
What happened?

Real-time and forensic data. Global-Roam (ez2view, NEMreview), gridIQ. Fast and accurate.

Well served — don't compete
Future tense
What might happen?

Long-horizon projections. Aurora, Endgame, Cornwall Insight. Prices, mix and loss factors to 2050 under named scenarios.

Well served — don't compete
Subjunctive
What would have happened?

What would this unit have earned but for that constraint? What did that closure actually do to price, as opposed to what coincided with it? How much of this curtailment is the grid?

This is where I work

A forecast gives you a price. A counterfactual gives you why — the only form you can argue in a negotiation, defend in an investment committee, or size a contract against.

The differentiation

Five things I couldn't find sold anywhere else.

  • The counterfactual has two bounds, and the gap is market power.

    Re-clear at cost: ~$1–2/MWh. Re-clear at what was actually offered: $14–21/MWh. Both real, neither alone the answer. The difference separates physical substitution from bidding conduct — a decomposition a single price number structurally cannot give you.

    Liddell 2022–23 · Hazelwood 2017 · 227-unit stack

  • Curtailment split by cause, and it overturns the usual conclusion.

    Everyone reports volume. Splitting network from economic shows network flat near 1 TWh since 2021 while economic rose 5.2×. In 2025, 85% was a price decision, not a grid limit. That inverts the investment implication.

    217 semi-scheduled units · monthly, per unit

  • Every cost traces to a published AEMO workbook cell.

    169 units priced from AEMO's own heat rates, station fuel prices and variable O&M, each carrying its source. Glass box, not calibrated black box. Doing it reversed the NSW coal order and recovered the country's largest brown-coal station from a silent name-matching bug.

    Coal coverage 99.5–100% in NSW, QLD, VIC

  • The reproduction test is published before the claim.

    Coal agreeing to the gigawatt-hour, both price series in all five regions to the dollar — published first. Cheap to pass, expensive to fake, and it turns "trust my model" into "check my arithmetic against the market operator's".

    Q2 2026 · 10 of 10 on price · coal to 0.00%

  • The failures are published too.

    A better weather dataset that turned out worse. A spike classifier that beats a naive rule by 1.1%. A hydro hypothesis that made the model worse when tested. A registry field wrong by 83×. A figure of mine that came from a hard-coded array. Every vendor here publishes forecasts; I haven't found one that publishes the validations it failed.

And where I have no advantage

  • Forward MLFs are occupied ground. Cornwall and Aurora both sell one. I don't forecast MLFs.
  • Bid-stack visualisation isn't novel. ez2view has a Bid Explorer; gridIQ advertises reconstruction. I use offers for counterfactual clearing, not to be first to show them.
  • No real-time product. For intraday, ez2view and gridIQ are the tools.
  • Battery benchmarking belongs to Modo. Their index is the reference.
  • No WEM coverage. gridIQ covers both markets; Aurora models both.
  • No live offer feed. AEMO publishes bids next day. "Live" would be false; next-day is the truth.
  • No track record. I'm new. Which is exactly why the reproduction test and the error bands exist — they're what a new entrant can offer instead of a reputation.
