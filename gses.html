02 — Automation / RPA Pre-production

CER Form Filler — 150 fields, never submitted.

Browser automation that opens a Clean Energy Regulator draft submission and fills every field from one canonical inspection record — about 150 fields that were keyed by hand, per site. It never clicks Submit: a human reviews and submits. That is a design constraint, not an oversight. Field maps were grounded in real templates and validated read-only against 50 live drafts before anything was written.

03 — Reporting / BI Built

QLD Government SSFR reporting — written by code, not by hand.

Reporting for Supercharged Solar for Renters under the Queensland Government Energy Programs Inspectorate: programme overview and scheduling pipeline, contractor and equipment allocation, and inspection outcomes with pre- and post-rectification tranche matrices. The report isn't clicked together — Python authors the data model, report JSON, theme and logo, so it regenerates instead of decaying when the person who built it is unavailable.

04 — Sales / CRM Delivered

Sales automation — guardrails in the code.

An account-based workflow: seed the CRM with target companies, confirm decision-makers manually, enrich business emails, and draft personalised intros into Outlook. Three guarantees are enforced in code rather than in a policy document — no automated LinkedIn actions, no auto-send, nothing leaves as a draft you haven't read. Outreach tooling that can send on its own is a reputational liability, and the constraint belongs where it can't be forgotten. Packaged and delivered to the Managing Director.

05 — Desktop utility Packaged

Photo evidence tool — defensible metadata.

A desktop app that corrects capture timestamps and applies watermarks to battery inspection photos, so field evidence carries consistent metadata when it reaches the regulator. Small problem, real consequence: a photo whose timestamp can't be trusted is a photo that can be challenged. Handles HEIC and HEIF alongside JPG and PNG, because that is what phones actually produce, and ships as a one-click executable so inspectors never see a terminal.

What it saved

83 minutes a site, 1,066 sites.

Per-inspection back-office time, before and after. Volumes come from the live workbooks; the minute figures are reasoned estimates, and I've labelled them as such rather than presenting them as measured.

What is still manual, and should be On-site capture still takes one to two hours per site and residential report writing is still a person. Those are the two largest remaining costs, and neither is a good automation candidate — one is physical inspection, the other is professional judgement being written down. Automating the wrong half of a process is how you end up with a fast pipeline producing work nobody trusts.
