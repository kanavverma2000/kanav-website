What we found

Six findings the market
hasn't priced in.

Gas — displacement, not weather

The 30% gas collapse happened in six hours a day.

Total sent-out energy was flat at +0.5%, so gas was displaced, not switched off. 76% of the 963 GWh decline sits between 16:00 and 21:00, and 78% of the battery growth is in the same six hours. The kill shot: midday gas rose 30 GWh, because charging batteries adds load. Weather is a level effect — it cannot do both.

Curtailment — cause attribution

Curtailment grew 5×. Network constraints didn't.

Network curtailment has been flat at roughly 1 TWh a year since 2021. Economic curtailment — the plant choosing not to generate — is up 420%. In 2025, 85% of curtailed renewable energy was a price decision, not a grid limit. That inverts the usual transmission-build conclusion.

Capture — locational divergence

Best and worst asset in the NEM: same region.

Coopers Gap Wind captured 106.6% of the Queensland average price in 2025. Darling Downs Solar, in the same region, captured 12.9% — an eight-fold spread inside one settlement price. Fleet medians: wind 72.1%, solar 39.8%. No regional price curve tells you which one you are buying.

Counterfactual — two bounds

A coal closure is worth $1/MWh or $20/MWh. Both are true.

Re-clearing the market at what plant costs gives ~$1–2/MWh. Re-clearing at what participants actually bid gives $14–21/MWh. The gap between the two bounds is the markup component — the part that comes from conduct rather than physics. A single number cannot show you that.

Supply curve — structure

The bid stack barely moves. That's why prices swing.

The offer ladder is identical to the previous trading day in 92.8% of unit-days, and band volumes are unchanged in 98.6% of consecutive intervals. But the curve is violently non-linear: the median step between used bands is $0 and the maximum is $15,795. Volatility is demand crossing a cliff — and since 2009 the cheap middle has thinned 61%.

Adequacy — coincidence, not average

The worst weather year is not the worst reliability year.

Across 36 reconstructed weather years, 1992 has the worst mean resource in the record and produces zero unserved energy. 2009 is mid-pack on average and breaches the reliability standard by 25×, because it holds the third-deepest 72-hour renewable drought. Sampling by average finds the dangerous year only by luck.

Scope

Built, serving, and honestly bounded.

What ARIA does

  • Per-unit curtailment with an economic-versus-network cause split
  • Merit-order counterfactual clearing, with cost and conduct bounds published separately
  • Per-unit revenue capture at five-minute granularity, benchmarked to the correct time-weighted denominator
  • Coal closure impact modelling from per-unit costs traced to AEMO's own workbook
  • 36 weather years of reconstructed wind and solar across 286 projects
  • Five-region capacity-expansion modelling with transmission priced inside the optimisation
  • Battery dispatch, cycling and event-based valuation
  • Quantile price-path scenarios with pre-dispatch bias correction

What ARIA does not claim

  • No AI price forecasting — the scenario engine is an explicit merit-order clear with published assumptions
  • No forward marginal-loss-factor product — Cornwall Insight and Aurora already sell one
  • No spike-prediction edge — ours beats a naive rule by 1.1%, so it is not sold
  • No real-time monitoring — ez2view and gridIQ are the tools for that
  • No automated bidding — running an optimiser and benchmarking one is a conflict
  • No Western Australian market coverage
  • No interval-level accuracy on the conduct-based clear — only the mean is readable
