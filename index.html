Ten of ten. The headline fell $66.06 against AEMO's stated −$66.

Why it isn't trivial AEMO's headline is a time-weighted average over five-minute dispatch periods. Its separate volume-weighted series is weighted by initial supply plus total intermittent generation — not total demand. Use total demand, the obvious guess, and you run $1–2/MWh high in every region. That one wrong choice quietly invalidates any capture benchmark built on it.
What I found

Six results the market
hasn't priced in.

+30 GWh

Midday gas rose while evening gas collapsed

Gas fell 30% over the year. AEMO reports the evening window and names batteries. It doesn't report that midday output went up — charging adds load. Weather is a level effect. It can't do both.

Total sent-out flat at +0.5%. Substitution 1:1 at 16:00 and 17:00.
85%

Curtailment is a price decision, not a grid limit

Network curtailment has sat near 1 TWh a year since 2021. Economic curtailment is up 420%. The growth is market structure, not transmission — so your exposure is to what gets built around you.

212 semi-scheduled units, 2021–2025.

Best and worst asset in the NEM, same region

Coopers Gap Wind captured 106.6% of the Queensland average in 2025. Darling Downs Solar captured 12.9%. About 100 km apart, same settlement price. No regional curve tells you which one you bought.

125 assets over 150 GWh. Wind median 72.1%, solar 39.8%.

The worst weather year caused no blackouts

1992 has the worst mean resource in 36 years and returns zero unserved energy. 2009 is mid-pack and breaches the standard 25-fold, because it holds the third-deepest 72-hour drought. Adequacy is coincidence, not average.

Scope

What it does, and
what it doesn't.

Does

  • Per-unit curtailment with an economic-versus-network cause split
  • Merit-order counterfactual clearing, cost and conduct bounds published separately
  • Per-unit revenue capture at five-minute granularity, against the correct time-weighted denominator
  • Closure impact from per-unit costs traced to AEMO's own workbook cells
  • 36 weather years of reconstructed wind and solar across 286 projects
  • Five-region capacity expansion with transmission priced inside the optimisation
  • Battery dispatch, cycling and event-based valuation
  • Scenario price paths with pre-dispatch bias correction

Doesn't

  • No AI price forecasting — the scenario engine is an explicit merit-order clear with published assumptions
  • No forward loss-factor product — Cornwall Insight and Aurora already sell one
  • No spike-prediction edge — ours beats a naive rule by 1.1%, so it isn't sold
  • No real-time monitoring — ez2view and gridIQ are the tools for that
  • No automated bidding — running an optimiser and benchmarking one is a conflict
  • No Western Australian coverage
  • No interval-level accuracy on the conduct clear — only the mean is readable
