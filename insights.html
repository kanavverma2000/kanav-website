“$3.70 against $1.00 — a Queensland scenario”

Run as the Analysis feature in AltEnergy Watts News, pages 8–9, credited to ARIA Energy. Same grid, same year, same prices — and a Queensland wind farm banks $3.70 from a megawatt-hour where a solar farm banks $1.00.

The mechanism is the effective revenue factor: capture rate multiplied by loss factor. Wind 103.8% × 96.1% = 99.8%. Solar 28.9% × 94.0% = 27.1%. A solar farm at $45/MWh needs the flat regional price to average $166/MWh before energy revenue covers it. Queensland's averaged $84.89.

Selected work

Why capture fell, and whether it was forecastable.

Beijing Energy International (Australia) watched dispatch-weighted capture across three NSW assets slide from about 85–90% to roughly 75%, and asked what caused it. I pulled five-minute SCADA and price data across the full operating history: Gullen Range fell 84% to 67%, Biala 80% to 70%, Wollar Solar sat at 25%. The causes were VRE cannibalisation, negative prices hitting 25.1% of NSW intervals, a duck curve that deepened from 0.29 to 0.17, and batteries taking 40% of evening-peak price setting. It was forecastable 12 to 18 months ahead — so I built the probability-weighted forward model to 2031 across seven scenarios.
