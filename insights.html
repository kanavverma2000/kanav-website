“$3.70 against $1.00 — a Queensland scenario”

Run as the Analysis feature in AltEnergy Watts News, pages 8–9, credited to ARIA Energy. Same grid, same year, same prices — and a Queensland wind farm banks $3.70 from a megawatt-hour where a solar farm banks $1.00.

The mechanism is the effective revenue factor: capture rate multiplied by loss factor. Wind 103.8% × 96.1% = 99.8%. Solar 28.9% × 94.0% = 27.1%. A solar farm at $45/MWh needs the flat regional price to average $166/MWh before energy revenue covers it. Queensland's averaged $84.89.

“Why did our capture rate fall, and could we have seen it coming?”

A NSW portfolio owner — Beijing Energy International (Australia) — watched dispatch-weighted capture across three assets slide from about 85–90% to roughly 75%, and asked two questions. What caused it, and was it forecastable.

1. What actually happened

I pulled five-minute SCADA and price data across the full operating history of all three assets, December 2019 to January 2026. The deterioration is real, and it accelerated.

Wollar has data only from late 2024. Public DUIDs, public prices — anyone can reproduce this.

2. Why
  • VRE cannibalisation. NSW average VRE output doubled in four years — 957 MW in Jan 2021 to 2,171 MW in Jan 2025. More generation in the same hours.
  • Negative prices. NSW went from ~0.2% of intervals in 2020 to 25.1% in Q4 2025. Gullen Range now generates 19% of its output at negative prices. Wollar Solar, 51%.
  • The duck curve deepened. Solar-hours price over evening-peak price fell from 0.29 in 2021 to 0.17 in 2025 — $40.3/MWh midday against $236.6 at the peak.
  • Batteries took the peak. Setting the marginal price in 40% of evening-peak intervals by Q4 2025, up from under 5% a year earlier.
3. Could it have been forecast? Yes — 12 to 18 months ahead. The negative-price trend was visibly exponential; a regression on quarterly data flags it by mid-2023. The solar-to-peak spread compressed 0.29 → 0.27 → 0.23 across 2021–2024, which makes 2025's 0.17 highly predictable. Rolling three-month capture fell below 80% from mid-2023 and never recovered. The connection pipeline was public the whole time.
4. Then the forward model

The follow-on question was where it goes. I built a probability-weighted forward capture model to 2031 across seven scenarios — ISP Step Change as the base at 35%, plus accelerated VRE, coal life extension, BESS saturation, demand uplift, regulatory delay and a combined stress case.

The central result: capture keeps falling under every scenario. Eraring's April 2029 closure gives a temporary price uplift and does not reverse the trend. Wind capture runs 69.3% today to 43.5% by 2031; the solar asset from 23.3% to 6.3%. The coal-extension case is the counter-intuitive one — keeping 8,240 MW online suppresses wholesale prices and makes the capture floor lower, not higher.

How I write them

Rules I set after
reading my own data.

I pulled the engagement on all 59 posts and let it decide the format rather than guessing. Some of what it said was unflattering.

What the data said to do
01
Open with a bare Australian figure. Number alone on line one, named on line two. Australian-number opens averaged 2,617 impressions against 577 for foreign ones — about 4.5×.
02
Provenance first, number second, mechanism third. "I processed 5.1 million dispatch intervals to answer one question." That line is the whole difference between analysis and commentary.
03
Name real assets, DUIDs and constraint IDs. Manildra, Coopers Gap, LD01–04, Q_NIL_STRGTH_CKWF. Specificity is the credibility.
04
Close with one question I genuinely can't answer from my own data, addressed to a specific role. The post that did that and credited a named person got 25 comments on 1,383 impressions — my highest engagement rate.
05
Publish the negative result. Including my own. Almost nobody in this market does, and it consistently reads above median.
What the data said to stop
  • Foreign-number cold opens. My four worst posts all opened with a Germany, UK, Texas or California figure. Average 6 reactions. The material is fine — as a second beat, never the first line.
  • Hashtag stuffing. 20–30 per post, some mid-sentence, which breaks the prose. My highest-reach post used 8. Now 3–5, at the end.
  • Generic closers. "Any thoughts?" does nothing when no real question is being asked.
  • Curiosity-gap hooks. "Here's what the ISP isn't telling you." My own data doesn't reward them either.
  • Mid-sentence caps and multiple exclamation marks. Sharp is on-voice. Melodramatic isn't.
  • Publishing the same post twice. I did that once. Process failure, not style.
The standing rule Re-derive every figure the morning it goes out. No placeholder numbers, ever. A figure read off a rendered chart is not a figure from the model — I learned that when a curtailment projection I published at 84% turned out to come from a hard-coded array while the model said 78.4%.
