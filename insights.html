The mechanism, and the method

Two things sit between megawatt-hours produced and dollars received: capture rate, and the loss factor on the trip down the wires. Multiply them and you get the effective revenue factor.

Wind: 103.8% capture times 96.1% MLF is 99.8% effective. Solar: 28.9% times 94.0% is 27.1%. A solar farm at $45/MWh needs the flat regional price to average $166/MWh before energy revenue covers it. Queensland's averaged $84.89.

51.5% of Queensland solar energy settled at zero or below last year. For wind it was 12.5%. Method: 7 wind farms, 37 solar farms, 10.4 TWh, calendar 2025, energy-weighted, regional time-weighted price as the denominator. Energy revenue only, so no FCAS, no LGCs, no contract positions.

Both closures, and the risk

Hazelwood, 2017. 1,600 MW of brown coal closed on 29 March with under five months' notice. Victorian wholesale prices rose about 85% year on year, calendar average $47.17 to $90.85. Victoria flipped from net exporter to net importer for the first time in a decade, with summer imports up from 181 to 915 GWh. Gas generation rose about 500% over the following summer. The peer-reviewed causal estimate puts the upper bound at $24.02/MWh and $6,527 million NEM-wide.

The part that matters commercially: remaining coal generators captured $3.47 billion in incremental revenue through bidding. That is the conduct effect, and it is the same thing my two-bound counterfactual is built to separate.

Yallourn, 2028. 1,480 MW, confirmed for 30 June 2028. Three things differ: seven years of notice rather than five months, Victorian renewable share up from about 13% to 43%, and battery storage from zero to 773 MW with 2,326 MW building. Base case is a $12 to 18/MWh uplift against Hazelwood's roughly $50.

The risk nobody prices: Eraring, 2,880 MW in NSW, closes April 2029, ten months after Yallourn. That is 4,360 MW of NEM coal gone inside a year, and if replacement capacity slips it becomes an inter-regional contagion problem rather than a Victorian one.

Validated against AEMO, ACCC, AER and peer-reviewed literature. Price data from 6.07 million NEMWeb settlement records, 2015 to 2025.

The diagnosis, and the leading indicators

Three NSW assets slid from about 85 to 90% dispatch-weighted capture to roughly 75%. I pulled five-minute SCADA and price data across the full operating history: Gullen Range 84% to 67%, Biala 80% to 70%, Wollar Solar at 25%.

Four causes. VRE output in NSW doubled in four years, 957 MW to 2,171 MW. Negative prices went from about 0.2% of intervals to 25.1% in Q4 2025, and Wollar now generates 51% of its output at negative prices. The duck curve deepened from 0.29 to 0.17, $40.3/MWh midday against $236.6 at the peak. And batteries took 40% of evening-peak price setting, up from under 5% a year earlier.

The leading indicators were all public. The negative-price trend was visibly exponential by mid-2023, the solar-to-peak spread compressed 0.29 to 0.27 to 0.23 across 2021 to 2024, and rolling three-month capture fell below 80% from mid-2023 and never recovered.
