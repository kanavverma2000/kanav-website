It is missing one tense.

The frame

Three questions.
Two of them are crowded.

Every product in this market answers one of three questions about the National Electricity Market. Two are served by mature, well-funded incumbents and there is no point competing there. The third is the one participants actually get asked in board papers, and it is the one nobody packages.

Past tense
What happened?

Real-time and forensic market data. Global-Roam (ez2view, NEMreview), gridIQ. Dispatch, prices, bids, constraints, rendered fast and accurately.

● Well served — don't compete
Future tense
What might happen?

Long-horizon fundamental projections. Aurora Energy Research, Endgame Economics, Cornwall Insight Australia. Prices, capacity mix and loss factors to 2050 under named scenarios.

● Well served — don't compete
Subjunctive
What would have happened?

The counterfactual. What would this unit have earned but for that constraint? What did that closure actually do to price, as opposed to what coincided with it? How much of this curtailment is the grid, and how much is the market?

● This is where ARIA works

The distinction matters commercially because it is the difference between a number and an attribution. A forecast tells you a price. A counterfactual tells you why the price was what it was — the only form in which the answer can be argued in a negotiation, defended in an investment committee, or used to size a contract against a specific risk.

The field

Real-time & forensic

Global-Roam — ez2view / NEMreview

Data acquisition, analysis and visualisation for organisations with significant NEM exposure — real-time monitoring plus after-the-fact forensics. Over 50 widgets including a Bid Explorer and bid stack charts, unit and station dashboards, and constraint-equation tooling. NEMreview is the historical query and trending product.

Overlap with ARIA: substantial on history and bid visibility. ez2view genuinely has bid-stack tooling — ARIA does not claim otherwise. The difference is that ez2view shows the stack; ARIA re-clears it.
Live data & AI delivery

gridIQ

Live NEM and WEM market data across six regions and every dispatch interval, with bid stack reconstruction, a rebid monitor, curtailment analysis, PPA and IRSR spread tools, a price forecast, and an AI assistant covering prices, generation, carbon, FCAS, constraints and gas.

Overlap with ARIA: the closest direct competitor, and the only one covering both NEM and WEM. Bid reconstruction and curtailment analysis both overlap. ARIA's separation is cause attribution and counterfactual clearing, not coverage.
Long-horizon fundamentals

Aurora Energy Research

Australian Power Market Service — wholesale price forecasts across six named scenarios in five regions to 2050, with capacity mix, generation and interconnector flows. A separate quarterly MLF and grid add-on gives annual marginal loss factors to 2050 under two scenarios for all operational and committed wind and solar.

Overlap with ARIA: none on history; total on forward loss factors. This is why ARIA does not sell MLF forecasting.
Dispatch simulation

Endgame Economics

A detailed simulation of market dispatch producing half-hourly forecasts of pool prices and plant output, three house scenarios refreshed every six months, delivered as a report plus raw half-hourly demand, dispatch and interconnector outcomes in CSV over a 20–25 year horizon.

Overlap with ARIA: they simulate dispatch forward at half-hourly resolution — genuinely deep modelling. ARIA re-clears backwards against what was actually bid. Different tense, different evidentiary standard.
Loss factors & benchmark curve

Cornwall Insight Australia

Network Forecasts (AUS) carries a forward MLF curve for all current NEM generators and selected renewable-energy-zone connection points, built off a 30-minute unit-level dispatch profile from their Benchmark Power Curve, delivered as an annual databook by state, zone and unit.

Overlap with ARIA: forward MLF is theirs, and it is a real product. ARIA treats per-asset MLF forecasting as occupied ground.
Storage benchmarking

Modo Energy

The ME BESS AUS NEM Index — a standardised independent benchmark for battery revenues and operations across the five NEM regions, with marginal loss factors incorporated into every revenue calculation, plus a periodic BESS revenue forecast.

Overlap with ARIA: battery benchmarking is theirs and they are the reference. ARIA covers batteries as one technology among all of them, not as a specialised index.

The differentiation

Five things ARIA does that we could not find sold elsewhere.

  • The counterfactual has two bounds, and the gap between them is market power.

    Closing a coal unit and re-clearing the market at what plant costs to run gives roughly $1–2/MWh. Re-clearing the same market at what participants actually offered gives $14–21/MWh. Both are real; neither alone is the answer. The difference separates the physical substitution effect from the bidding-conduct effect — a decomposition that a single price number, from any vendor, structurally cannot provide. It is also why the conduct bound is published as a range with its correlation stated, not as a point estimate.

    Liddell 2022–23 · Hazelwood 2017 · 227-unit national offer stack

  • Curtailment is split by cause, and the answer overturns the usual conclusion.

    Everyone reports curtailment volume. Splitting it into network-constrained versus economic shows that network curtailment has been flat at about 1 TWh a year since 2021 while economic curtailment rose 5.2×. In 2025, 85% of curtailed renewable energy was a price decision, not a grid limit. That inverts the investment implication: the developer's exposure is to what gets built around them, not to what gets built to them — and that is a different capital allocation.

    217 semi-scheduled units · monthly, per unit, 2019–2026

  • Every cost in the stack traces to a published AEMO workbook cell.

    Short-run marginal cost is computed per unit — 169 of them — from AEMO's own heat rates, station fuel prices and variable operating costs, each carrying its source so any served number can be walked back to the cell it came from. It is a glass box, not a calibrated black box. Doing this reversed the New South Wales coal order relative to the capacity-based proxy the engine used before, and recovered the country's largest brown-coal station from a silent name-matching bug.

    169 units · coal coverage 99.5–100% in NSW, QLD, VIC

  • The reproduction test is published before the claim.

    ARIA reproduces AEMO's own quarterly headline — coal agreeing to the gigawatt-hour, and both the time-weighted and volume-weighted price series in all five regions to the dollar — and publishes that first. It is a cheap test to pass and an expensive one to fake, and it converts "trust our model" into "check our arithmetic against the market operator's".

    Q2 2026 · 10 of 10 on price · coal to 0.00%

  • The failures are published too.

    A higher-resolution Australian weather dataset that turned out worse than the global one. A spike classifier that beats a naive rule by 1.1%. A hydro hypothesis that made the model worse when tested. A registry field that is wrong by 83× on one unit. A figure of our own that came from a hard-coded array rather than the model. Each one is on the research page with its measurement. Every other vendor in this market publishes forecasts; we have not found one that publishes the validations it failed.

The other side of the ledger

Where ARIA has no advantage, and says so.

A differentiation claim is only worth reading if the same document is willing to name the places it does not hold.

  • Forward marginal loss factors are occupied ground. Cornwall Insight Australia and Aurora both sell a forward MLF curve across the NEM. ARIA does not forecast MLFs and does not intend to compete there.
  • Bid-stack visualisation is not novel. ez2view has a Bid Explorer and bid stack charts; gridIQ advertises bid stack reconstruction. ARIA's use of offer data is for counterfactual clearing, not for being the first to show it.
  • No real-time product. ARIA is a history-and-counterfactual platform. For intraday monitoring, ez2view and gridIQ are the tools, and there is no point pretending otherwise.
  • Battery benchmarking belongs to Modo. Their index is the market reference and incorporates loss factors in every revenue calculation. ARIA covers storage as one technology, not as a specialised benchmark.
  • No Western Australian coverage. The WEM is not in the data. gridIQ covers both markets; Aurora models both.
  • Long-horizon projection is not the strength. Endgame simulates half-hourly dispatch over 25 years and Aurora runs six scenarios to 2050. ARIA's system model exists to test structural questions, not to compete as a forward price curve.
  • No live offer feed. AEMO publishes bids the following day. "Live" would be a false claim; next-day is the truth.
  • No track record. ARIA is new. There is no multi-year forecast history to point at, which is precisely why the reproduction test and the published error bands exist — they are what a new entrant can offer instead of a reputation.
Price

The market prices on data breadth, not seats.

ARIA follows the same structure — gated on analytical breadth with seats effectively free inside a band — because the buyers are small analyst teams inside large organisations, where per-seat pricing punishes exactly the internal spread you want. The counterfactual layer is what justifies the top tier; dataset count alone would not.
