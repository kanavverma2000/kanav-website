Where it fits

Three questions.
Two are crowded.

Market analytics answers one of three questions. Two are crowded with good tools. The third is the one people actually get asked in board papers.

Past tense
What happened?

Real-time dashboards and after-the-fact forensics. Several mature products do this well, and they're fast and accurate.

Well served elsewhere
Future tense
What might happen?

Long-horizon projections. Prices, generation mix and loss factors out to 2050 under named scenarios, sold by established consultancies.

Well served elsewhere
Subjunctive
What would have happened?

What would this unit have earned but for that constraint? What did that closure actually do to price, as opposed to what coincided with it? How much of this curtailment is the grid?

This is where I work

A forecast gives you a price. A counterfactual gives you why: the only form you can argue in a negotiation or defend in an investment committee.

What it covers

Nine things in, five out.

Drawn deliberately narrow. Everything on the right is something the platform could plausibly reach for and doesn't, either because it's well served already or because I can't do it honestly yet.

In scope

  • Historical dispatch and price, five-minute resolution
  • Bid and offer stack, as actually submitted
  • Per-unit capture benchmarking
  • Curtailment measured per unit, per month
  • Curtailment attributed to cause, economic against network
  • Counterfactual re-clearing of the dispatch stack
  • Cost and conduct bounds, separated
  • A published AEMO reproduction, before any claim
  • Published error bands, and published failures

Not in scope

  • Real-time monitoring, intraday tooling is a solved problem
  • Long-horizon projection to 2050
  • Forward marginal loss factors
  • Western Australia, the NEM only
  • Battery revenue indexing, covered only as one technology among several

Bold rows are the ones that took the longest to build and are the reason the rest is worth having.

And where it stops

Seven things it doesn't do
  • Forward loss factors are occupied ground. Established products already sell an MLF curve to 2050. I don't forecast MLFs.
  • Showing the bid stack isn't novel. Existing tools display it well. I use offers to re-clear dispatch, not to be first to draw them.
  • No real-time product. For intraday decisions, dedicated monitoring tools are the right answer.
  • No battery revenue index. A standardised benchmark already exists and is the reference. I cover storage as one technology among several.
  • No Western Australian coverage. The NEM only.
  • No live offer feed. AEMO publishes bids next day. "Live" would be false; next-day is the truth.
  • No track record. I'm new. Which is exactly why the reproduction test and the error bands exist, they're what a new entrant can offer instead of a reputation.
