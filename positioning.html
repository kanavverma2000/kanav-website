Who actually sells what

From each vendor's own product pages, August 2026.

Real-time & forensic

Global-Roam, ez2view / NEMreview

Real-time monitoring plus after-the-fact forensics. 50+ widgets including a Bid Explorer and bid stack charts, unit and station dashboards, constraint-equation tooling.

Overlap: substantial. ez2view genuinely has bid-stack tooling: the difference is it shows the stack, I re-clear it.
Live data & AI delivery

gridIQ

Live NEM and WEM across six regions, bid stack reconstruction, rebid monitor, curtailment estimates, PPA and IRSR spreads and an AI assistant. The price forecast they surface is AEMO's own pre-dispatch, relayed; their methodology page says they do not model the market.

Overlap: the closest competitor on coverage, and they publish their pricing too. They estimate curtailment volume; their API returns no cause field. And they rule out the modelling category entirely, which is exactly where I sit.
Long-horizon fundamentals

Aurora Energy Research

Price forecasts across six scenarios in five regions to 2050. A separate quarterly add-on gives annual MLFs to 2050 under two scenarios for operational and committed wind and solar.

Overlap: total on forward loss factors. Which is why I don't sell them.
Dispatch simulation

Endgame Economics

Half-hourly forecasts of pool prices and plant output, three house scenarios, raw outcomes in CSV over a 20–25 year horizon, refreshed quarterly. They also ship live NEM tooling (pd4castr, DAEG), a bid-stack product (pdBidr) and a battery model.

Overlap: more than I first credited. They have live tooling and a bid-stack product alongside the projection. They simulate forward; I re-clear backwards against what was actually bid.
Loss factors

Cornwall Insight Australia

Network Forecasts (AUS) carries a forward MLF curve for all current NEM generators and selected REZ connection points, as an annual databook by state, zone and unit.

Overlap: forward MLF is theirs. Occupied ground.
Storage benchmarking

Modo Energy

The ME BESS AUS NEM Index: a standardised benchmark for battery revenues and operations across five regions, with MLFs in every revenue calculation.

Overlap: battery benchmarking is theirs and they are the reference. I cover storage as one technology.

The differentiation

Five things I couldn't find sold anywhere else.

  • The counterfactual has two bounds, and the gap is market power.

    Re-clear at cost: ~$1–2/MWh. Re-clear at what was actually offered: $14–21/MWh. Both real, neither alone the answer. The difference separates physical substitution from bidding conduct: a decomposition a single price number structurally cannot give you.

    Liddell 2022–23 · Hazelwood 2017 · 227-unit stack

  • Curtailment split by cause, and it overturns the usual conclusion.

    Global-Roam's annual Generator Statistical Digest estimates the economic and network split too, so this is not mine alone, and I've marked them accordingly. The difference is delivery: theirs lands once a year in a report, mine is per unit per month in the platform. Either way the finding stands. Network sits flat near 1 TWh since 2021 while economic rose 5.2×; in 2025 85% was a price decision, not a grid limit.

    217 semi-scheduled units · monthly, per unit

  • Every cost traces to a published AEMO workbook cell.

    169 units priced from AEMO's own heat rates, fuel prices and variable O&M, each carrying its source. Glass box. Doing it reversed the NSW coal order and recovered the country's largest brown-coal station from a silent name-matching bug.

    Coal coverage 99.5–100% in NSW, QLD, VIC

  • The reproduction test is published before the claim.

    Coal to the gigawatt-hour, both price series in all five regions to the dollar, published first. Cheap to pass, expensive to fake, and it turns "trust my model" into "check my arithmetic".

    Q2 2026 · 10 of 10 on price · coal to 0.00%

  • The failures are published too.

    A better weather dataset that turned out worse. A spike classifier beating a naive rule by 1.1%. A registry field wrong by 83×. A figure of mine that came from a hard-coded array. Every vendor here publishes forecasts; I haven't found one that publishes the validations it failed.

And where I have no advantage

  • Forward MLFs are occupied ground. Cornwall and Aurora both sell one. I don't forecast MLFs.
  • Bid-stack visualisation isn't novel. ez2view has a Bid Explorer; gridIQ advertises reconstruction. I use offers for counterfactual clearing, not to be first to show them.
  • No real-time product. For intraday, ez2view and gridIQ are the tools.
  • Battery benchmarking belongs to Modo. Their index is the reference.
  • No WEM coverage. gridIQ covers both markets; Aurora models both.
  • No live offer feed. AEMO publishes bids next day. "Live" would be false; next-day is the truth.
  • No track record. I'm new. Which is exactly why the reproduction test and the error bands exist, they're what a new entrant can offer instead of a reputation.
